Pentatonic Boxes

The minor pentatonic is five notes, and its formula works like a function that takes the root as its argument. Pick a key and the same shapes slide to a new place on the neck. Tap any dot to see what it is.

Dot labels

The top line is the thin high E string and the bottom line the thick low E, the way tab reads.

The formula

Tap an interval to light up every place it appears. The number under each is how many frets above the root it sits.

Reading a neck diagram

  • Horizontal lines are strings, vertical lines are frets. Further right is higher up the neck.
  • Red is the root, the note the scale is named after.
  • Dark dots are the other scale notes.
  • Small dots in the wood are the inlay markers at frets 3, 5, 7, 9 and 12. Landmarks, not notes.

Recognising box 1

  1. Count the dots on each string. A pentatonic box has two on every string; three on a string means a seven-note scale.
  2. Find the roots: the low and high E strings at the same fret, and the D string two frets higher.
  3. The outer strings stretch three frets; the middle ones stretch two.
