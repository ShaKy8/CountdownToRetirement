CAGED Explorer

Five open chord shapes slide up the neck and lock together like tiles, always in the order C, A, G, E, D. Each shape shares a root note with the next, so together they map one key across the whole fretboard.

Pick a shape to study it. The number is the fret it starts on.

Why learn it

1

Any chord, five places. Every major chord can be played once per shape, so one is always near your hand.

2

Solos that follow the chords. Play a shape's chord tones one at a time and the line outlines the harmony.

3

Scales with a landmark. Each shape anchors a scale position, so the pentatonic and the major scale hang off a chord you already know.

4

Short moves between chords. When the chord or the key changes, reach for the shape that is already under your fingers. Try the planner below.

The system belongs to the guitar: the shapes are the open chords of standard tuning. On a keyboard every key has its own look, so there is nothing to slide.
